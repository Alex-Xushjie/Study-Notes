# Style Guide

[Home](README.md) · [Templates](templates/README.md) · [Index rules](INDEX-RULES.md)

Write entirely in clear English for working network engineers. Organize by scene, physical object, technical concept, and professional expression. Do not include translations in entries, metadata, or navigation.

## Visual-first chapter design

Begin each visual chapter with one large scene. Use short leader lines and numbered markers to identify visible objects. Directly below the image, list corresponding English headwords in number order with short definitions. Follow this list with natural example sentences and usage. Use focused illustrations for individual physical entries when useful, and link them from the word list. All images and wording must be original or appropriately licensed; do not reproduce a publisher's exact page design, illustrations, or text.

## Entry structure

Each entry contains a stable ID, headword, type, chapter, aliases, tags, draft status and update date. Include a short English definition, grammar and usage, meaningful distinctions or limitations, common combinations, at least two original work examples, related-entry links, and a source or explicit note that a dedicated source is pending.

Physical entries explain what an object is and how it is recognized. Technical entries explain its scope, mechanism, and boundaries. Expression entries explain intent, tone, and a reusable sentence pattern. Use nouns in their standard singular form and retain standard capitalization for proper names and abbreviations.

## Visuals

Use original or appropriately licensed illustrations. Place image files in `assets/`, give descriptive English alternative text, and link the image to relevant entries. Images must show the object clearly and be identified as illustrative when layouts vary. Do not use a scene image as evidence of a technical requirement; verify exact details with authoritative sources.

## Editing and review

Use specific, natural workplace English and avoid ambiguous pronouns. Distinguish dimensions, location, status and cause. Specify units, time zones, and product or protocol versions where needed. Prefer authoritative sources, and record the relevant source and scope. Drafts become reviewed only after independent technical and language checks; a link check is not an editorial review.
